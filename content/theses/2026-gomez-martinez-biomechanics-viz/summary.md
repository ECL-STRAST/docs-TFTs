This Bachelor Thesis presents the development of CHLOE (Clinical Helper for Locomotion Objective Evaluation), a web-based application designed for the visualization and analysis of biomechanical data. CHLOE is integrated into the LibreMotion project, which aims to democratize the capture, storage, and analysis of biomechanical data, with a particular focus on data obtained from low-cost devices.

The primary goal of CHLOE is to improve clinical access to movement data recorded in C3D format, the standard in biomechanics laboratories for storing marker trajectories and complementary analog signals, such as electromyography (EMG). Standard C3D viewers often require specialized software that is complex to configure and non-intuitive; furthermore, their functionalities rarely align with the specific needs of physiotherapists. To address this, a solution based on open-source technologies was implemented. CHLOE is a browser-based, easy-to-use tool that requires virtually no configuration and focuses on the tasks most frequently performed by physiotherapists in clinical practice. Its ease of use and low learning curve facilitate seamless integration into existing clinical workflows.

CHLOE automatically recognizes C3D data corresponding to the Plug-in Gait protocol, the most common standard for full-body motion capture (MoCap). However, it is not limited to this model and can visualize any set of markers. This flexibility allows for the integration of diverse data sources, ranging from optical motion capture (OMC) systems used in research to low-cost devices such as inertial sensors or extended reality (XR) headsets.

Technically, the project utilizes Python and the ezc3d library to transform binary C3D data into JSON structures. These files are consumed by a visualization engine developed with Three.js, which enables the rendering of animated point clouds, the construction of dynamic skeletons (for Plug-in Gait data), and the mapping of 3D avatars from Mixamo. Plotly.js is used to generate interactive graphs of user-selected data, while the graphical interface also allows for tracking specific marker trajectories. Additionally, the tool integrates analog data management to evaluate parameters such as ground reaction forces and muscle activation.

The application was deployed using Docker containers to ensure system portability and straightforward installation. Finally, the system was validated through usability testing with physiotherapy professionals, who confirmed its utility in clinical and educational settings, highlighting its potential for motor imagery and objective biomechanical analysis.
